No Surprises

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Nobody likes surprises on the final bill. That's why St. Cloud Water Heater quotes every job upfront, after a full diagnostic that looks at the heater's age, fuel type, and capacity, the burner assembly or heating elements, the anode rod and relief valve, and the venting and supply lines. You'll know what's wrong, what it takes to fix, and what it costs before you say yes.

Most water heater failures give quiet warnings long before the tank quits. Keep an eye out for hot water with a rusty tint, a rumbling tank, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, or showers that go cold sooner than they used to. Behind those symptoms are usually a thick layer of sediment, a spent anode rod, or a worn dip tube. An early service call at the first sign often costs far less than mopping up after a ruptured tank.

How old is your water heater? The date code on the label usually gives it away. Once a tank passes the 10-year mark, it's worth planning a replacement before the current one forces the issue. As a rough guide, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. Our team will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is nearing the end.

Preventive Care

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing year-round. Regular service prevents failures before they start, flushes out mineral sediment, and catches small faults while they're still inexpensive to fix. At St. Cloud Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your specific equipment, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a burner and flue check on gas units, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a thermostat setting check.

Most homes do best with one flush a year, and houses on hard water may need two. We adjust the schedule to the age of your heater, how hard your household runs it, and local water quality, which controls how fast sediment accumulates and how long an anode rod lasts.

The savings show up faster than most people expect. A flushed tank transfers heat more efficiently, a replaced anode rod can add years to the tank, and a tested relief valve is ready to do its job if pressure ever climbs too high. Next to the cost of an early replacement, a maintenance plan is a bargain.

Right-Sized Hot Water

A Water Heater Sized for Your Actual Household

Proper sizing matters more than most homeowners realize. Before recommending a new heater, St. Cloud Water Heater works through the details that determine your household's peak demand: how many people and bathrooms you have, back-to-back showers, laundry and dishwasher timing, and how cold the water entering the house gets. That gives you a unit with enough first-hour capacity or GPM to handle your morning rush without paying to heat water you'll never use.

Picking a new water heater is about more than tank size. Our technician will compare tank models with tankless units, and gas with electric or heat pump, walking through operating costs, venting and gas line work, electrical capacity, and the installation itself. Tankless is a strong fit for many families, but it often needs a larger gas line and new venting, and a properly sized tank is often the simpler, lower-cost option. The decision is yours, based on your home and budget.

Repairs We Handle

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

No hot water, a burner that refuses to light, a rumbling, sediment-heavy tank, a weeping relief valve: every one of these problems has its own cause and calls for a different repair. St. Cloud Water Heater diagnoses the exact fault before replacing anything, whether it's an electric tank that trips its breaker, a thermocouple that no longer holds the pilot, a broken dip tube mixing cold water into the hot, or a tankless unit throwing error codes.

The right repair depends entirely on what actually failed. On electric tanks, it's usually an element or thermostat. On gas units, it's more often a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure spikes get solved with an expansion tank and a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and we handle leak repairs at the drain valve and fittings before they spread. We also check the thermostat setting, since water above 120°F can scald kids and older family members in seconds. A tank leaking from the steel itself can't be patched, and we'll tell you so. And if you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility right away.
